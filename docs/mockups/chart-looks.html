Four ways to draw the same trade

Every panel below renders the identical 65 bars — one full AreaTrap cycle: a 20-minute profile window, a shallow break under the value area low on draining volume, absorption that refuses to make a new low, and the close back inside that arms the long. Only the drawing changes. Drag the scrubber to move all four charts through the cycle together; the profile builds live while the window is open, then freezes.

A

Session box

The reference look. Histogram anchored inside the window, box around it, the three levels projected right to the live bar.

Reads well

The window is unmistakable — you can see exactly which bars built the area and which are trading against it. Closest to the chart you sent.

Costs

The histogram sits on top of the first third of the price action. On a 10-minute window that is most of the window.

B

Projected band

The value area as a filled zone thrown forward across the whole chart. The histogram fades out the moment the window closes.

Reads well

Inside-or-outside is answered at a glance, which is the only question the entry rule asks. Cleanest of the four to actually trade off.

Costs

Once the histogram is gone you lose the shape of the auction — you can no longer see whether the POC was a spike or a plateau.

C

Split delta

Each profile row split by aggressor: sell-initiated volume to the left of the spine, buy-initiated to the right. Absorption becomes visible in the profile itself.

Reads well

The only look where the absorption step is shown rather than inferred: heavy sell volume piled at a price the market refused to leave.

Costs

Twice the ink and it needs volumetric or tick data to be honest. Drawn from bar OHLCV alone the split is an estimate, and an estimate here is a lie.

D

Right ladder

Profile pinned to the right gutter where it never touches price, plus a status block that states the machine's state in words.

Reads well

Price action is completely unobstructed, and you can read what the strategy is doing without inferring it from shapes. Best for Playback debugging.

Costs

The profile no longer lines up in time with the bars that built it, so the link between window and area is something you have to know rather than see.

What I need from you

Pick a look — or mix them, they are parameters, not architectures. Then these are the design decisions that change what gets built, in the order they matter.

  1. The R : R above is 0.90, and that is structural, not bad luck. Entering on the close back inside costs you the whole reclaim distance, while the stop still sits under the break low. Options: cap how deep a break may go and still qualify; enter with a resting limit at the value area low instead of on the close; or target beyond the far side. Which one do you want to try first?
  2. Does the window rebuild on the clock or after the trade? You said the next window starts once the trade finishes. That means the cycle drifts off the clock and the strategy is blind while it rebuilds. The alternative is a fixed grid where the profile always rebuilds on the half hour and a trade can straddle the boundary.
  3. How long may the area stay armed before it is stale? If no setup appears, at some point a frozen 10-minute area from 40 minutes ago stops describing anything. A timeout that forces a rebuild is one line of code and I would default it on — the question is the number.
  4. Minimum area width before the cycle is allowed to trade. The trade targets the far side of the area. If the area is only a few points wide, that target does not clear costs and the cycle should sit out rather than take it.
  5. How hard is absorption allowed to be? Look C draws it. Detecting it needs order flow, which restricts the strategy to Playback and live — never the Strategy Analyzer. If you want it optional, the strategy needs a cheap fallback mode that runs on bar volume alone.
